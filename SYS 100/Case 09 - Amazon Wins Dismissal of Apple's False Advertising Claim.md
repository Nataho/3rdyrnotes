# Case 09 — Amazon Wins Dismissal of Apple's False Advertising Claim

## Summary
A short legal news item: U.S. District Judge Phyllis Hamilton (Oakland, California) dismissed Apple's false advertising claim against Amazon over Amazon's use of "Appstore" for its Android software market. Apple's suit for trademark infringement and unfair competition continues, with a trial set for August 19. The case hinges on whether "app store" is a generic term Apple can't exclusively own.

## Storyline
1. The headline result: Amazon.com wins dismissal of Apple's false advertising claim over the online retailer's use of "app store" for Android device software.
2. The ruling: U.S. District Judge Phyllis Hamilton in Oakland, California grants Amazon's request to throw out one claim in Apple's lawsuit alleging trademark infringement and unfair competition over the Amazon Appstore for Android — a service begun in March selling apps for the Kindle Fire and Android devices.
3. What Apple sought: a court order blocking Amazon from using the term; a trial was scheduled for August 19. Amazon's defense: the words are a generic term Apple doesn't have exclusive rights to use.
4. Apple's theory: its APP STORE launched in 2008; Amazon's use was false advertising because it deceives customers into believing Amazon's service has the qualities of Apple's application store — potentially diverting Apple's revenue to Amazon.
5. Why it failed: Apple didn't show Amazon's advertising mimicked Apple's, failed to show any false statement, and presented no evidence customers were misled; the court "finds no support for the proposition that Amazon has expressly or impliedly communicated that its Appstore for Android possesses the characteristics and qualities" the public expects from Apple. The ruling covers only the false advertising claim — the trademark claims live on toward trial.
6. The genericness subplot: in 2008 Apple applied to the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office to register APP STORE; Microsoft opposed, calling the term generic; the Trademark Trials and Appeals Board put that opposition on hold pending this lawsuit.
7. The silence: Apple's spokeswoman declines comment; a call to Amazon's media line isn't immediately returned.

## Key Points
- **The dispute:** Apple launched its APP STORE in 2008 and sued Amazon (and Microsoft, in a separate trademark registration battle) over its "Amazon Appstore for Android," launched in March to sell apps for the Kindle Fire and Android devices.
- **The claim:** Apple argued Amazon's use was false advertising that deceived customers into thinking Amazon's service had the qualities of Apple's App Store, potentially diverting Apple's revenue.
- **The ruling:** Hamilton found no support for the idea that Amazon expressly or impliedly claimed its store had Apple's characteristics; Apple showed no false statement, no mimicking advertising, and no evidence any customer was misled. The dismissal covers only the false advertising claim; the remaining trademark claims go to trial.
- **The genericness fight:** Amazon argues "app store" is generic. Microsoft opposed Apple's 2008 application to register APP STORE with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office on the same grounds; the Trademark Trials and Appeals Board put that proceeding on hold pending this lawsuit.
