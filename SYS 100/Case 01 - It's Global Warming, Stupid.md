# Case 01 — It's Global Warming, Stupid

## Summary
An opinion piece arguing that Hurricane Sandy (Oct 2012) makes it impossible to keep ignoring climate change. While no single storm can be blamed on warming, climate change "amps up" the basic factors that supercharge storms — warmer oceans provide more energy, a warmer atmosphere holds more moisture, and Arctic ice melt contributes to the blocking-high pattern that pushed cold air south and collided with Sandy. The article frames this as "weather on steroids."

## Storyline
1. Hurricane Sandy's damage is laid out: 40+ U.S. deaths, up to $50B in losses, 8 million homes without power, hundreds of thousands evacuated, 15,000+ flights grounded, Lower Manhattan dark and underwater.
2. The standard caveat: no single storm can be blamed on climate change — and deniers exploit that complexity to dodge the discussion entirely.
3. Foley's tweet: the storm would have happened without climate change, but it's *stronger* because of it.
4. Pooley's steroids analogy: we can't credit steroids for any one Barry Bonds home run, but they helped him hit more, and farther — "weather on steroids."
5. The mechanism (Fischetti, Scientific American): warmer oceans supply more storm energy; a warmer atmosphere holds more moisture that gets drawn into storms and dumped on us.
6. Sandy's extra twist: the hurricane collided with cold Canadian air — pushed south by a blocking high over the Arctic that Cornell's Greene and Monger link to Arctic ice melt caused by global warming.
7. Munich Re's Oct 17 report: North America's weather-related loss events nearly quintupled from 1980–2011 ($1.06 trillion in losses) — outpacing Asia (4x), Africa (2.5x), Europe (2x), South America (1.5x); human-caused climate change is believed to contribute.
8. Corroborating extremes: July 2012 was the hottest U.S. month since records began in 1895; two-thirds of the continental U.S. was in drought.
9. What to do: Owen's *The Conundrum* argues a growth- and consumption-obsessed West will keep burning fossil fuels — efficient gadgets aren't enough.
10. The political landscape: climate was absent from the presidential debates; Romney mocked Obama's "slow the rise of the oceans" vow at the RNC; Obama answered by promising to mine more coal and drill more oil, with only modest climate progress (fuel-efficiency standards, coal-plant rules, doubled renewables — still under 15% of electricity).
11. Pragmatic proposals: natural gas as a bridge fuel (half coal's carbon); carbon pricing modeled on Alaska's oil dividends — cap-and-trade died in the Senate in 2010.
12. The global fix: the Durban agreement calls for a binding treaty by 2015, effective 2020 — but it needs penalties for non-compliance the U.S. has refused to support.
13. Sandy's second lesson: disaster response — Romney's "kill FEMA" remark looks untenable next to Christie's glowing review of the federal response to the storm.
14. Hardening infrastructure: surge protectors and subway waterproofing (Cuomo/Bloomberg), buried power lines (Rothkopf) — paid for by trimming DHS waste.
15. Closing diagnosis: Pew shows belief in warming down 10 points since 2006; the deniers' framing is winning, and the U.S. can't afford regular Sandy-size disruptions.

## Key Points
- **Sandy's toll:** 40+ U.S. deaths, up to $50B in losses, 8 million homes without power, 15,000+ flights grounded, Lower Manhattan flooded.
- **Munich Re data:** weather-related loss events in North America nearly quintupled from 1980–2011, with $1.06 trillion in losses — a faster rise than Asia, Africa, Europe, or South America.
- **Record conditions:** July 2012 was the hottest U.S. month since records began in 1895; two-thirds of the continental U.S. was in drought that summer.
- **Politics:** climate was absent from the presidential debates. Romney mocked Obama's 2008 "slow the rise of the oceans" pledge; Obama offered only modest steps (fuel-efficiency standards, coal-plant emission rules, doubled renewables — still under 15% of U.S. electricity).
- **Proposed path:** natural gas as a bridge fuel (half the carbon of coal), carbon pricing (cap-and-trade died in the Senate in 2010; Alaska's oil-dividend model suggested), a binding global treaty by 2015, stronger FEMA, and infrastructure hardening (surge protectors, waterproofing the NYC subway, burying power lines).
- **Public opinion:** a Pew poll found two-thirds of Americans see "solid evidence" of warming — down 10 points since 2006.
