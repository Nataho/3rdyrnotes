# Case 04 — Airport Security Is Killing Us

## Summary
A cost-benefit critique of the TSA. The threat of terrorism on U.S. soil is statistically tiny, yet airport security consumes billions and makes flying miserable — which indirectly kills people by pushing travelers onto far more dangerous roads. The article argues the real failure is a system of misaligned incentives among politicians, media, and voters who punish officials for visible plane incidents but ignore larger invisible risks.

## Storyline
1. The busiest travel week of the year begins; the TSA — created after 9/11 — has largely outlived its usefulness as the homeland terror threat recedes, and its main role now is making plane trips unpleasant, pushing people onto considerably more dangerous roads.
2. The threat quantified: fewer than three dozen of the 150,000 U.S. murders between 9/11 and end-2010 were Islamic-extremist; the odds a U.S. resident dies in a terrorist attack are 1 in 3.5 million; worldwide (outside Iraq/Afghanistan), 200–400 deaths a year — the same as U.S. bathtub deaths.
3. The apparatus: an ~$8B budget leaves the TSA with too many officers and not enough to do — 2011's "Top Good Catches" were 1,200 firearms and one batch of C4 (found only on a return flight), alongside a G.I. Joe doll's plastic rifle and a light saber; the agency caught no terrorists boarding U.S. flights (Schneier).
4. The bill comes due: the attacks caused $55B in direct damage and $123B in economic impact — but security spending and the wars total $3,105B, with ~$580B of homeland-security spending from 2002–11.
5. The evaluation gap: most of it was implemented "with little or no evaluation" (Rand's Thomson), and the National Academy of Sciences found DHS lacked adequate risk-analysis capability — the money is fired "completely blindly."
6. The lethal side effect: to make flying as dangerous as driving, a four-plane 9/11-scale disaster would have to occur *every month*; Cornell estimates the post-9/11 shift from air to road travel caused 242 extra driving deaths per month, and baggage screening's ~5% cut in passenger volume over five years added 100+ road deaths.
7. Assigning blame: not the TSA workers (they follow incentives rewarding slavish rule-following over common sense), not Homeland Security officials (they're avoiding the greater backlash of doing nothing) — the blame lies with politicians, the media, and the electorate, who punish a single fatal plane incident while ignoring deadlier car crashes, gun homicides, and bathtub accidents.
8. The prescription: this Thanksgiving, don't beef up security — slashing the TSA would leave more people alive to spend future holidays with loved ones.

## Key Points
- **Threat vs. response:** of 150,000 U.S. murders between 9/11 and end-2010, Islamic extremism accounted for fewer than three dozen. Since 2000, the odds an American dies in a terrorist attack were 1 in 3.5 million. Worldwide (outside Iraq/Afghanistan), extremist terrorism kills 200–400 people a year — about the same as U.S. bathtub deaths.
- **Spending:** the TSA's budget is ~$8B. Total 9/11-related costs: $55B in direct damage, $123B economic impact, but $3,105B in security spending and wars; ~$580B went to homeland security in 2002–11 — largely implemented "with little or no evaluation" (Rand Corp.; National Academy of Sciences found DHS lacked adequate risk-analysis capability).
- **Track record:** the TSA's top 2011 catches were 1,200 firearms and one batch of C4 — plus a G.I. Joe doll's 4-inch plastic rifle and a light saber. It caught no terrorists boarding planes in the U.S.
- **Lethal side effect:** to make flying as dangerous as driving, a 9/11-scale four-plane disaster would have to happen *every month* (American Scientist). Cornell researchers estimate the post-9/11 shift from air to road travel caused 242 extra driving deaths per month; baggage screening alone cut passenger volume ~5% over five years, causing 100+ additional road deaths.
- **Incentive problem:** TSA workers follow rewards for rule-compliance over common sense; Homeland Security officials avoid the backlash of "doing nothing" — but the ultimate blame lies with politicians, media, and the electorate.
- **Conclusion:** slashing the TSA would save more lives than it would ever cost.
