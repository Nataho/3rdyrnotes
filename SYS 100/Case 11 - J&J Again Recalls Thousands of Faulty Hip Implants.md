# Case 11 — J&J Again Recalls Thousands of Faulty Hip Implants

## Summary
Johnson & Johnson's DePuy Orthopaedics unit recalled all 7,500 "Adept" brand all-metal hip replacement systems shipped worldwide between 2004 and September 2011, because national joint registries showed revision-surgery rates well above expectations. The recall comes 2½ years after J&J's recall of two other metal-on-metal hips (the ASR line), which triggered thousands of lawsuits and a first trial over metal poisoning — part of a pattern of 30+ product recalls across J&J since 2009.

## Storyline
1. The news: Johnson & Johnson has again recalled thousands of hip implants — 2½ years after the problem-plagued giant recalled two other types of its artificial hips.
2. The recall: DePuy Orthopaedics pulled the "Adept" brand all-metal total hip replacement system starting last month, because a higher-than-expected percentage needed replacement — revision surgeries usually required when an artificial joint starts causing pain, difficulty walking, or other problems.
3. The scope: only the top part of the system — the femoral ball that fits into the hip socket; all 7,500 Adept implants shipped worldwide between 2004 and September 2011, when J&J sold the product back to its original developer (DePuy had bought the rights in 2009).
4. The geography: sold in Germany and 20 other countries — but never in the U.S.
5. The trigger: J&J notified surgeons and hospitals on January 14 after reviewing national joint-replacement registries in two countries — the U.K. registry found 12.1% of patients needed replacement within seven years; Australia's found 7.1% within three years.
6. The unknown: DePuy's spokeswomen didn't know how many of the recalled implants were actually implanted in patients; anyone experiencing problems should contact their doctor.
7. The leak: the German newspaper Handelsblatt reported the recall Thursday; J&J notes it excludes the Adept Hip Resurfacing Femoral Components product.
8. The pattern: J&J — the world's biggest health-care products provider — has issued more than 30 recalls since 2009, mostly nonprescription medicines (adult and children's Tylenol and Motrin) but also epilepsy prescriptions and contact lenses, for causes including wrong active-ingredient levels, glass or metal shards in liquid medicines, and nauseating packaging smells.
9. The regulator: the company operates under increased FDA scrutiny while rebuilding one nonprescription medicine factory from the ground up and upgrading others; the recalls and lost sales have cost well over $1 billion.
10. The original sin: in August 2010, J&J recalled two types of DePuy ASR metal hip implants after high failure rates — those recalls led to thousands of lawsuits by U.S. patients.
11. The courtroom: three weeks before this announcement, an LA jury began hearing the first ASR case to reach trial — former North Dakota prison guard Loren Kransky, 64, whose implant allegedly shed black metal flakes causing a metal poisoning that could have killed him; J&J's lawyer counters that Kransky had many pre-existing ailments.
12. The twist: J&J shares rose 15 cents to close at $75.81 after hitting $76.09 intraday — an all-time high for the stock (FactSet).

## Key Points
- **The recall:** announced internally Jan 14 after registry reviews; covers only the femoral ball component. Sold in Germany and 20 other countries but never in the U.S.
- **Failure data:** a U.K. registry found 12.1% of patients needed implant replacement within seven years; an Australian registry found 7.1% within three years.
- **The litigation backdrop:** the August 2010 recall of two DePuy ASR metal hips led to thousands of U.S. lawsuits. In the first trial (Loren Kransky, former prison guard), plaintiffs alleged black metal flakes caused metal poisoning that could have killed him; J&J argued his ailments were pre-existing.
- **Pattern of problems:** J&J, the world's biggest health-care products provider, has issued 30+ recalls since 2009 — Tylenol and Motrin (adult and children), epilepsy drugs, contact lenses — for causes including wrong active-ingredient levels, glass/metal shards in liquids, and nauseating packaging odors.
- **Regulatory pressure:** the company operates under increased FDA scrutiny while rebuilding one nonprescription medicine factory from scratch and upgrading others; recalls and lost sales have cost it well over $1 billion.
- **Market reaction:** oddly, J&J shares closed at an all-time high of $75.81 on the day the recall was reported.
