# Case 1: It's Global Warming, Stupid — System Dynamics Analysis

## 1. Hurricane Influences & Variable Quantifications

Based on the Case 1 text, a hurricane operates within an interconnected system of atmospheric, oceanic, and socio-economic variables.

### A. Variables That Influence a Hurricane (Inputs)
* **Fossil Fuel Burning & Carbon Emissions:** The combustion of coal, petroleum, and fossil energy that traps heat in the atmosphere.
  * *Quantification:* Natural gas emits ~50% less carbon than coal when burned for power; new fuel-efficiency standards aim to cut vehicle greenhouse emissions by 50% by 2025.
* **Global Warming & Ocean Temperatures:** Thermal energy absorbed by the oceans and atmosphere.
  * *Quantification:* Recorded as global thermal spikes, such as July 2012 being the hottest month in the continental U.S. since record-keeping began in 1895.
* **Atmospheric Moisture Retention:** The volume of water vapor held by warmer air and drawn into storms.
  * *Quantification:* Percentage increase in atmospheric humidity resulting in intense precipitation dumped during severe weather.
* **Arctic Icemelt & Blocking High Patterns:** Melting Arctic sea ice altering atmospheric jet streams and sending cold air southward to collide with warm ocean storms.
  * *Quantification:* Extent of Arctic ice loss contributing to atmospheric pressure blocking patterns.

### B. Variables Influenced by a Hurricane (Outputs & Impacts)
* **Human Mortality & Displacement:** Fatalities and forced evacuations resulting from storm surges and urban flooding.
  * *Quantification:* At least 40 U.S. deaths and hundreds of thousands of evacuated residents during Hurricane Sandy.
* **Economic Losses & Structural Damage:** Total financial destruction, grounded commercial transportation, and power utility failure.
  * *Quantification:* $50 billion in expected losses from Hurricane Sandy; $1.06 trillion in total weather disaster losses in North America from 1980 through 2011; 8 million homes without power; >15,000 flights grounded.
* **Weather-Related Loss Event Frequency:** The rate of catastrophic weather events over time.
  * *Quantification:* A nearly quintupled (5x) increase in weather-related loss events in North America over three decades.
* **Political Will for Climate Action & Infrastructure Spending:** Public and governmental urgency to fund disaster response (FEMA), waterproof transit, or regulate carbon emissions.
  * *Quantification:* Percentage of public belief in global warming (67% in October 2012, down 10 points from 2006) and renewable energy generation share (<15% of U.S. electricity).
# Reinforcing Loop
![[Pasted image 20260817201753.png]]
## Prioritization of Short-Term Economic Growth -> Fossil Fuel Burning & Carbon Emissions
> Placing high value on immediate economic growth and consumer gratification drives increased burning of fossil fuels (coal and oil) to power factories, offices, and transport.

## Fossil Fuel Burning & Carbon Emissions -> Global Warming & Ocean Temperatures
> Higher carbon emissions trap atmospheric heat, causing global atmospheric and ocean temperatures to rise.

## Global Warming & Ocean Temperatures -> Hurricane Intensity & Storm Strength
> Warmer oceans provide more energy to storms, while a warmer atmosphere holds more moisture, increasing hurricane strength and precipitation.

## Hurricane Intensity & Storm Strength -> Economic Losses & Infrastructure Damage
> Stronger hurricanes cause massive economic destruction, power grid shutdowns, and infrastructure flooding.

## Economic Losses & Infrastructure Damage -> Prioritization of Short-Term Economic Growth
> Severe economic strain causes politicians and the public to focus heavily on short-term economic recovery, encouraging promises to mine more coal and drill more oil rather than invest in long-term climate action.

# Balancing Loop
![[Pasted image 20260817201820.png]]

## Hurricane Intensity & Storm Strength -> Economic Losses & Infrastructure Damage
> Increased storm energy leads to catastrophic economic damage, flooding cities and destroying power systems.

## Economic Losses & Infrastructure Damage -> Political Will for Climate Action & Regulations
> High-profile disasters like Hurricane Sandy force climate change back onto the political agenda, building political support for disaster preparedness, renewable energy transitions, and carbon taxation.

## Political Will for Climate Action & Regulations -> Fossil Fuel Burning & Carbon Emissions
> Regulatory measures (such as power plant emission caps, fuel-efficiency rules, and cap-and-trade systems) reduce overall fossil fuel burning.

## Fossil Fuel Burning & Carbon Emissions -> Global Warming & Ocean Temperatures
> Lowering carbon emissions slows the rate of heat trapped in the atmosphere and ocean upper layers.

## Global Warming & Ocean Temperatures -> Hurricane Intensity & Storm Strength
> Reduced ocean and atmospheric warming limits the thermal energy available to fuel supercharged tropical cyclones.
