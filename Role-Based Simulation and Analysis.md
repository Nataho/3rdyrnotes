# University Student System - Integration Project Planning

## 1. Project Overview
**Project Name:** University Student System Integration Project
**My Role:** Project Manager

## 2. Roles, Responsibilities, and Concerns

### Project Manager (My Role)
* **Responsibilities:** Oversee the entire integration project from initiation to closure. Manage the allocated money, ensure timelines align with the academic calendar, and act as the bridge between technical teams, administration, and end-users. 
* **Concerns:** Staying within strict budget constraints, scope creep, ensuring all departments are aligned, and preventing timeline delays that could disrupt the upcoming semester.

### IT Systems Lead
* **Responsibilities:** Design the technical architecture, handle data migration from legacy systems, ensure system security, and guarantee the infrastructure can handle high traffic (e.g., thousands of students accessing the system simultaneously during enrollment).
* **Concerns:** Technical debt from old legacy systems, server crashes during peak traffic, data breaches, and integrating disjointed databases (e.g., financial and academic records).

### Business Unit Manager (e.g., University Registrar/Finance Admin)
* **Responsibilities:** Ensure the new system correctly enforces university policies, handles financial transactions accurately, and provides necessary reporting tools for the staff.
* **Concerns:** Disruption to daily administrative operations during the transition, data corruption during migration, and the possibility that the allocated money won't cover essential operational features.

### End-user Representative (Student & Faculty Liaison)
* **Responsibilities:** Advocate for the needs of students and teaching staff. Test the system for usability, accessibility, and intuitive design.
* **Concerns:** A steep learning curve, confusing interfaces, system downtime during critical periods (like submitting grades or paying tuition), and lack of proper onboarding.

## 3. Potential Organizational Issues

* **Resistance to new technology:** Many university staff members and veteran professors may be accustomed to the legacy system or paper-based processes. They may view the new system as an unnecessary complication rather than an upgrade.
	> we can use a a paper based protocol. in the paper based protocol, what the veteran staff will do is they get the necessary printed papers, fill those up and scan it with an interpreter software so that the physical document is read and turned into digital automatically. they can also edit the document if there are some things the interpreter could not read but if they still wouldn't get it, there will be another staff that will help them with the protocol if they need help with it.

* **Poor communication between departments:** The IT department might use technical jargon that the Business Unit Managers (Registrar/Finance) do not understand. Conversely, IT might not fully grasp the intricate academic rules and prerequisites needed for the system.
	> We can have a policy dealing with that. First for the poor communication with the Professionals. in the policy we will state that when talking to an employee with the same profession they can talk technically but when dealing with employees with different a profession, they have to talk in english rather than technically to avoid jargon. Conversely, for IT not getting the full grasp of academic rules and prerequisites. we will ask the university registrar the academic rules, the prerequisites, the student handbook and other things the system may need.

* **Conflicting priorities:** The IT Lead may want to delay the launch to ensure perfect system stability and handle high traffic seamlessly, whereas the Project Manager and Business Unit Manager are pressured to launch the system immediately before the next enrollment period begins.
	> Since the university has an academic calendar, there are no excuses to delay the launch of the system before enrollment however, what we can do is to launch the system with a limit to its traffic, let the student be advised to refresh the page if it doesn't load and let the IT lead start a branch of the System so that they can address the high traffic issue. after fixing the high traffic issue, we can update the system quetly when there is little traffic like midnight
* 
* **Lack of user training:** With thousands of students and hundreds of staff members, providing adequate training is a massive logistical challenge. Without it, users will struggle, leading to a flood of support tickets and frustration.
	> Different groups need different levels of training staff who use the system daily need hands-on sessions, while students and faculty just need simple guides for the features they'll actually use. Skipping this step means people get confused, support teams get overwhelmed, and users may avoid the new system together, even if it works fine technically. That's why training should be planned and budgeted early, not added in at the last minute.
* 
* **Budget/timeline constraints:** The project is bound by a fixed allocated money pool and a rigid academic calendar. Any delays or requirements for additional funds will require difficult trade-offs, such as cutting planned features (like a mobile app version) to launch on time.
